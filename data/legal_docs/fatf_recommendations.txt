IMPORTANT LEGAL STATUS

FATF Recommendations are international standards, not Vietnamese legislation.
They should NOT be described as "Vietnamese law", "a Vietnamese regulation",
or as a substitute for Vietnamese legal instruments.

For DigitalAsset Guard:
- Vietnamese legal conclusion -> cite the relevant Vietnamese law/regulation.
- FATF conclusion -> explicitly identify the relevant FATF Recommendation,
  Interpretive Note, Guidance, or FATF report.
- System-derived graph/risk signals -> label as SYSTEM_DERIVED.
- Do not turn a FATF indicator into a claim that Vietnamese law automatically
  requires an STR for that pattern.

1. RECOMMENDATION 1 — RISK-BASED APPROACH
============================================================

CORE PRINCIPLE
--------------
Countries, financial institutions and relevant obliged entities should identify,
assess and understand money laundering and terrorist financing risks and apply
risk-based measures proportionate to the risks identified.

For virtual assets and VASPs, risk assessment and mitigation should consider:
- the nature of virtual asset activities;
- the activities and operations of VASPs;
- the interaction between VAs/VASPs and traditional financial institutions;
- relevant geographic, customer, product/service and delivery-channel risks.

DIGITALASSET GUARD APPLICATION
------------------------------
Use Recommendation 1 as international AML context for:
- risk scoring;
- customer risk assessment;
- transaction monitoring;
- graph-based risk analysis;
- prioritisation of high-risk cases.

IMPORTANT:
A DigitalAsset Guard risk score is a SYSTEM_DERIVED assessment.
It must not be presented as a FATF-defined score.

2. RECOMMENDATION 10 — CUSTOMER DUE DILIGENCE (CDD)
============================================================

CORE CDD MEASURES
-----------------
Financial institutions must undertake customer due diligence in the situations
specified by the FATF Standards, including:
- when establishing business relations;
- when carrying out applicable occasional transactions;
- when there is a suspicion of ML/TF;
- when there are doubts about the veracity or adequacy of previously obtained
  customer identification data.

CDD includes, as applicable:
1. Identifying the customer and verifying identity using reliable,
   independent source documents, data or information.
2. Identifying the beneficial owner and taking reasonable measures to verify
   the beneficial owner's identity.
3. Understanding and, where appropriate, obtaining information on the purpose
   and intended nature of the business relationship.
4. Conducting ongoing due diligence and scrutiny of transactions throughout
   the relationship to ensure consistency with the institution's knowledge of
   the customer, their business and risk profile, including source of funds
   where appropriate.

VIRTUAL-ASSET CONTEXT
---------------------
For VASPs, FATF guidance applies the relevant preventive measures in
Recommendations 10 to 21 in the same general manner as for financial
institutions, with specific qualifications for virtual-asset activity.

The FATF's VA guidance identifies USD/EUR 1,000 as the relevant occasional-
transaction CDD threshold for VA transactions under the FATF framework,
rather than the general USD/EUR 15,000 threshold.

IMPORTANT:
This FATF threshold is an international FATF standard/context. It must NOT be
automatically substituted for a threshold in Vietnamese law.

DIGITALASSET GUARD APPLICATION
------------------------------
Relevant inputs:
- KYC identity;
- beneficial ownership;
- customer profile;
- stated purpose;
- transaction history;
- source of funds where available;
- geographic exposure;
- consistency between customer profile and observed blockchain activity.

3. RECOMMENDATION 15 — NEW TECHNOLOGIES, VIRTUAL ASSETS AND VASPs
============================================================

CORE PRINCIPLE
--------------
FATF revised Recommendation 15 and its Interpretive Note to apply AML/CFT
measures to virtual assets and virtual asset service providers.

COUNTRY-LEVEL EXPECTATIONS
--------------------------
Countries should:
- identify and assess ML/TF risks arising from relevant VA activities and
  VASPs;
- take appropriate measures to manage and mitigate those risks;
- require VASPs to be licensed or registered as required by the FATF framework;
- subject VASPs to appropriate regulation, supervision or monitoring;
- ensure that relevant AML/CFT preventive measures apply to VASPs.

VASP SCOPE
----------
FATF defines a VASP by reference to specified activities performed as a
business for or on behalf of another natural or legal person, including
relevant exchange, transfer, safekeeping/administration, and financial-service
activities connected with an issuer's offer or sale of a virtual asset.

Do not invent a broader VASP definition from examples alone.

VIRTUAL ASSET SCOPE
-------------------
FATF's framework applies to "virtual assets" as defined in the FATF Standards.
Examples commonly discussed in FATF materials include crypto-assets such as
Bitcoin and other relevant digital assets, but the exact legal classification
must follow the FATF definition and the applicable national law.

P2P / UNHOSTED WALLETS
----------------------
FATF guidance identifies peer-to-peer transactions and transactions involving
unhosted wallets as areas requiring risk-based attention.

IMPORTANT:
An unhosted wallet is NOT automatically illicit.
Risk must be assessed using the surrounding facts and circumstances.

DIGITALASSET GUARD APPLICATION
------------------------------
Relevant system capabilities:
- wallet/entity risk assessment;
- sanctions screening;
- graph analysis;
- exposure analysis;
- transaction monitoring;
- risk-based investigation prioritisation.

4. RECOMMENDATION 16 — PAYMENT TRANSPARENCY / TRAVEL RULE
============================================================

CORE PRINCIPLE
--------------
Recommendation 16 establishes requirements concerning information that should
accompany qualifying transfers so that relevant information about the
originator and beneficiary is available and can support financial-crime
controls.

VIRTUAL-ASSET APPLICATION
-------------------------
For VA transfers, the FATF framework applies Recommendation 16 in a modified
form commonly referred to as the "Travel Rule".

Relevant information can include information necessary to identify the
originator and beneficiary and to support the required transfer of information
between obliged entities.

FATF guidance states that the Travel Rule applies to VASPs and other obliged
entities engaging in qualifying VA transfers.

THRESHOLD CAUTION
-----------------
FATF materials have used a USD/EUR 1,000 de minimis threshold for relevant
VA-transfer requirements in the implementation framework.

However, FATF updated Recommendation 16 in June 2025. Therefore, the exact
current technical requirements and implementation details must be checked
against the current FATF Recommendation 16 / Interpretive Note and applicable
national implementation.

DO NOT:
- treat the FATF Travel Rule as Vietnamese law;
- claim that every blockchain transaction is automatically subject to a
  Travel Rule obligation;
- convert the FATF threshold into a Vietnamese legal reporting threshold.

DIGITALASSET GUARD APPLICATION
------------------------------
Potential compliance checks:
- identify originator/beneficiary information where available;
- detect missing or inconsistent transfer information;
- distinguish VASP-to-VASP / obliged-entity transfers from ordinary
  self-hosted wallet activity;
- retain the distinction between FATF compliance context and Vietnamese law.

5. RECOMMENDATION 20 — REPORTING OF SUSPICIOUS TRANSACTIONS
============================================================

CORE PRINCIPLE
--------------
Financial institutions and relevant obliged entities should report promptly
to the financial intelligence unit when they suspect, or have reasonable
grounds to suspect, that funds are the proceeds of a criminal activity or are
related to terrorist financing.

KEY POINT
---------
Suspicious transaction reporting is not based only on a fixed transaction
value.

The relevant question is whether the facts give rise to suspicion or
reasonable grounds for suspicion under the applicable framework.

DIGITALASSET GUARD APPLICATION
------------------------------
The system can:
- identify suspicious patterns;
- aggregate evidence;
- explain risk factors;
- retrieve relevant legal/compliance sources;
- generate a draft STR;
- send the case for human review.

The final reporting decision must remain subject to the applicable legal and
institutional process.

6. FATF VIRTUAL ASSET RED FLAG INDICATORS (2020)
============================================================

PURPOSE
-------
FATF's 2020 Virtual Assets Red Flag Indicators report helps reporting entities,
FIUs, law enforcement and regulators identify potential ML/TF activity
involving virtual assets.

These indicators are NOT automatic proof of criminal activity.
A red flag should be assessed together with the customer's profile,
transaction context, legitimate economic rationale and other available
evidence.

SELECTED TRANSACTION-LEVEL INDICATORS
-------------------------------------
Examples include:
- transaction activity that is inconsistent with the customer's profile or
  expected activity;
- unusual transaction patterns or structuring;
- rapid movement of virtual assets through multiple addresses or services;
- use of mechanisms or services that can obscure transaction origin,
  destination or ownership;
- transactions involving jurisdictions or counterparties associated with
  elevated risk;
- activity involving multiple virtual assets or services without an apparent
  legitimate economic rationale;
- unusual patterns involving privacy-enhancing technologies or mixing
  services, where relevant to the facts;
- rapid movement of funds through multiple wallets or services.

GRAPH-RELEVANT INDICATORS
-------------------------
FATF's red-flag materials can provide contextual support for investigating:
- multi-hop movement of value;
- rapid movement across multiple wallets/services;
- transaction patterns designed to obscure source or destination;
- unusual clustering or movement of assets.

IMPORTANT:
Terms such as "fan-out", "fan-in", "chain-hopping", "circular flow" or
"mixer" may be useful as SYSTEM_DERIVED analytical labels, but they should not
be presented as if FATF had defined each label as a mandatory STR rule.

The 2020 FATF report contains examples and indicators; it does not create a
mechanical scoring formula.

============================================================
7. CUSTOMER / BEHAVIOURAL RED FLAGS
============================================================

Potential indicators include:
- reluctance or inability to provide satisfactory identification or
  information about the purpose of transactions;
- activity inconsistent with the customer's known profile or business;
- use of multiple accounts/wallets or jurisdictions without an apparent
  legitimate rationale;
- transactions that appear designed to avoid controls;
- unusual urgency or unexplained rapid conversion/movement of virtual assets;
- explanations that are inconsistent with transaction activity.

IMPORTANT:
These are investigative indicators, not automatic findings of money
laundering.

8. STABLECOINS, UNHOSTED WALLETS AND P2P
============================================================

CURRENT FATF CONTEXT
--------------------
FATF's 2025 targeted update identifies continuing illicit-finance risks
associated with virtual assets, including significant use of stablecoins by
illicit actors.

FATF's March 2026 targeted report specifically examines stablecoins and
peer-to-peer transactions involving unhosted wallets.

The report highlights the value of:
- stronger risk assessment;
- public-private cooperation;
- blockchain analytics and other investigative tools;
- measures addressing illicit activity involving stablecoins and unhosted
  wallets.

DIGITALASSET GUARD APPLICATION
------------------------------
This supports the project's use of:
- blockchain graph analytics;
- wallet exposure analysis;
- risk scoring;
- suspicious-pattern detection;
- evidence generation for investigators.

Do not infer that every stablecoin or unhosted-wallet transaction is
suspicious.

============================================================
9. FATF GUIDANCE FOR RISK-BASED APPROACH TO VA/VASP
============================================================

The 2021 FATF guidance provides implementation context for:
- understanding VA/VASP risks;
- licensing/registration and supervision;
- CDD;
- ongoing monitoring;
- suspicious transaction reporting;
- Travel Rule implementation;
- P2P and unhosted-wallet risk;
- information sharing and cooperation.

The guidance is intended to help countries and VASPs implement the FATF
requirements in the virtual-asset sector.

============================================================
10. 2025 TARGETED UPDATE — VA/VASPs
============================================================

FATF's June 2025 targeted update reports that jurisdictions have made progress
but continue to face implementation challenges relating to Recommendation 15.

The report highlights, among other issues:
- continuing illicit-finance risks involving stablecoins;
- fraud and scams;
- challenges in risk assessment and mitigation;
- the need for stronger implementation of AML/CFT controls;
- the importance of international cooperation;
- asset recovery and disruption of illicit networks.

Use this document as current contextual evidence, not as a substitute for
the text of the FATF Recommendations.

12. LEGAL RAG BOUNDARY RULES
============================================================

RULE 1:
FATF is an international standard-setting body. Do not describe FATF
Recommendations as Vietnamese legislation.

RULE 2:
When a question asks "theo pháp luật Việt Nam", prioritize Vietnamese legal
sources. FATF may be provided as international context, but it cannot replace
Vietnamese law.

RULE 3:
When a question asks "theo FATF", cite the relevant Recommendation,
Interpretive Note, Guidance or FATF report.

RULE 4:
Do not convert a FATF red flag into an automatic conclusion that a transaction
is money laundering.

RULE 5:
Do not convert a FATF red flag into an automatic STR requirement under
Vietnamese law.

RULE 6:
Graph features such as:
- fan-out;
- fan-in;
- hop distance;
- PageRank;
- centrality;
- circular-flow detection;
- exposure score;
- mixer exposure;
are SYSTEM_DERIVED features unless a source explicitly states otherwise.

RULE 7:
A DigitalAsset Guard risk score is SYSTEM_DERIVED and is not a FATF score.

RULE 8:
A Vietnamese reporting threshold must come from Vietnamese law/regulation,
not from a FATF threshold.

RULE 9:
The FATF Travel Rule is distinct from Vietnamese rules on electronic
transfers. Do not merge the two.

RULE 10:
Red flags are investigative signals. Final STR decisions require contextual
assessment and applicable legal/institutional procedures.
